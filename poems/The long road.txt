The long road

Imagine, for a moment, 
you're standing still, motionless,
on the side of a long straight road.

There's no cars, no people.
Just towering office blocks
lining each side as far as you can see.

So you start to walk, slowly, cautiously,
Inspecting the buildings as you pass.
Noticing everything's identical, sterile.

Inside the nearest building, atop the front desk. 
There's a lone brass nameplate.
That simply reads, A. Jones.

There's no one inside. 
There's no one to greet.
Just an empty seat.

You try to get in, 
pulling and pushing.
But the door doesn't budge.

So you continue on your way.
walking further and further down the road.
You pass ten buildings, then a hundred more.

Inside each and every one,
sits an empty chair
and a new name, on a small brass plate.

The road continues stretching,
the offices continue towering.
And you're here, on the road.

No where to turn,
no where to shelter.
A stranger amongst loneliness.
